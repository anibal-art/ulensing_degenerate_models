\title{Annual-parallax detectability in LSST microlensing\\
\large Empirical LRT calibration and current characterization}
\author{Roman--Rubin hidden-parallax analysis\\\small Coauthor analysis note}
\date{23 September 2026}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We study the intrinsic detectability of annual microlensing parallax in LSST-only
finite-source point-lens (FSPL) light curves using a likelihood-ratio statistic based on the
difference in best-fit $\chi^2$ between a no-parallax model and a parallax model.  The
experiment is deliberately idealized: the simulated photometric noise model is known,
nonlinear fits are truth-initialized, catalog-based blending is included in the materialized
light curves, and no unmodeled instrumental or astrophysical systematics are injected.  The
goal is therefore to isolate the information content of the sampled light curves from the
separate problem of global parameter-search performance on real data.

A dedicated $\Hnull$ population is used to calibrate the null distribution empirically, and
an $\Halt$ population is used to measure conditional parallax-detection efficiency after the
pre-fit microlensing detectability selection.  Under the current frozen two-fit policy, the
primary $\alpha=10^{-3}$ threshold is $\widehat c_{10^{-3}}=13.1598$ and the corresponding
population-averaged conditional power is $0.6891$.  These values describe the current frozen
procedure and are not yet treated as final paper numbers: the remaining high-priority checks
are the exact nesting/warm-start construction, the approximately global offset of the null
distribution from $\chi^2_2$, the behavior of the weakly constrained finite-source parameter,
and the stability of the null false-positive rate in physical event variables.

The present characterization already gives a robust qualitative picture.  Detectability is
controlled strongly by event timescale and true parallax amplitude; weak-parallax events show
a positive-definite amplitude bias while the signed North/East components remain nearly
unbiased; and the component covariance is conservative in both one and two dimensions.  This
note records the current results, their reproducible repository paths, and the specific
validation work required before a paper freeze.
\end{abstract}

\section{Study scope and scientific question}
% ============================================================

The goal is to determine when annual parallax is \emph{intrinsically distinguishable} in an
LSST microlensing light curve after the event has already passed the approved pre-fit
microlensing detectability selection.  The central question is not whether an event is
detectable as microlensing, but whether the sampled light curve contains enough information
to distinguish an FSPL model with annual parallax from the corresponding FSPL model without
annual parallax.

The scope is intentionally narrower than an end-to-end real-data detection pipeline.  We
assume known Gaussian photometric errors, use truth-initialized nonlinear fits to reduce the
impact of global search failures, include the catalog blending prescription used by the
simulation, and do not inject unmodeled systematics or correlated noise.  Truth initialization
is therefore a design choice: it is intended to separate the statistical information question
from the optimization-search question.

The primary statistic is
\begin{equation}
    T \equiv \dchi
    =
    \chi^2_{\Hnull}
    -
    \chi^2_{\Halt}.
\end{equation}
For exact global minima of nested models one must have $T\ge0$.  The current two-fit production
retains a small negative tail because the independently optimized $\Halt$ fit is not explicitly
anchored to the settled $\Hnull$ optimum.  Those events are treated as numerical nesting
violations, not as physical evidence against the larger model.

\statusbox{Current status.}{The results in this note are a reproducible snapshot of the frozen
production and characterization.  They are suitable for coauthor discussion, but the quoted
threshold and power remain provisional until the nesting/warm-start and null-scale checks in
Section~\ref{sec:open_checks} are completed.}

\section{Hypotheses and microlensing models}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Null hypothesis}

The null model is an FSPL light curve without annual parallax:
\begin{equation}
    \Hnull:
    \qquad
    \bm{\theta}_0
    =
    (t_0,u_0,\te,\rhoFS).
\end{equation}

The generating $\Hnull$ simulations are also produced without parallax. No artificial true
values of $\pen$ or $\pee$ are assigned to the $\Hnull$ population.

\subsection{Alternative hypothesis}

The alternative model is an FSPL light curve with annual parallax:
\begin{equation}
    \Halt:
    \qquad
    \bm{\theta}_1
    =
    (t_0,u_0,\te,\rhoFS,\pen,\pee).
\end{equation}

Thus the alternative contains two additional nonlinear parameters,
\begin{equation}
    \Delta k = 2.
\end{equation}

The parallax amplitude is
\begin{equation}
    \pi_{\rm E}
    =
    \left|\piE\right|
    =
    \sqrt{\pen^2+\pee^2}.
\end{equation}

% ============================================================
\section{Likelihood-ratio test}
% ============================================================

For Gaussian photometric uncertainties, the difference in best-fit $\chi^2$ plays the role of
twice the log-likelihood ratio:
\begin{equation}
    T
    =
    \chi^2_{\Hnull}
    -
    \chi^2_{\Halt}.
\end{equation}

Under regularity conditions, Wilks' theorem would suggest
\begin{equation}
    T \sim \chi^2_{\nu=2}
    \qquad
    \text{under }\Hnull.
\end{equation}

For two degrees of freedom,
\begin{equation}
    P(\chi^2_2 > T)
    =
    \exp(-T/2),
\end{equation}
and the asymptotic critical value at false-positive level $\alpha$ is
\begin{equation}
    c_{\alpha,\mathrm{Wilks}}
    =
    -2\ln\alpha.
\end{equation}

However, this problem contains nonlinear parameters, finite bounds, survey sampling,
pre-selection, and numerical optimization. We therefore do not assume that Wilks' theorem is
exact. The final calibration is empirical.

The calibration campaign processed candidate ranks $[0,300000)$ in deterministic order.

    N_{\rm materialized} &= 103\,937,\\
    N_{\rm not\ detectable} &= 195\,474,\\

\end{align}

% ============================================================
\section{Pre-fit detectability selection}
% ============================================================

The production selection is applied before the LRT fits and defines the population to which
the reported power applies. The frozen configuration requires:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item Rubin photometry only for the pre-fit gate;
    \item at least 10 total photometric points;
    \item at least 3 observed bands;
    \item a peak window of $\pm 1\te$;
    \item at least 5 points in the peak window;
    \item at least one point on each side of the peak within $\pm 1\te$;
    \item at least 6 points above the adopted $3\sigma$ criterion;
    \item minimum Asimov-like $\Delta\chi^2$ per point equal to 2;
    \item the no-parallax model as the reference model for the pre-fit detectability gate.
\end{itemize}

Consequently, every reported $\Halt$ detection fraction is a \emph{conditional} detection
efficiency among events that already pass this pre-fit selection. It is not the detection
fraction over all physical microlensing events in the parent Galactic population.

\section{Frozen production fitting policy}
\label{sec:policy}
% ============================================================

The production policy is intentionally minimal: every selected event receives exactly one
$\Hnull$ TRF fit and exactly one $\Halt$ TRF fit.  No differential evolution, global
multistart, continuation, rescue, or additional projected-null fit is included in the current
production statistic.

Because this experiment targets intrinsic detectability rather than blind real-data search,
truth values are used only to initialize nonlinear parameters; all fitted parameters remain
free.  The exact frozen policy is:

The covariance matrices stored in the compact exports are expressed in physical parameter
coordinates.  The $\Hnull$ covariance order is $(t_0,u_0,\te,\rho)$ and the $\Halt$ order is
$(t_0,u_0,\te,\rho,\pen,\pee)$.

\subsection{Production bounds}

The current production uses the truth-independent \texttt{production\_candidate} fit domain.
The bounds are
\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
parameter & production bound & construction\\
\midrule
$t_0$ & $[t_{\min}^{\rm obs},t_{\max}^{\rm obs}]$ & data-driven, zero extra margin\\
$u_0$ & $[-10,10]$ & global fixed bound\\
$\te$ & $[0.1,5\times10^5]\,$d & global fixed bound\\
$\rho$ & $[10^{-7},10]$ & global fixed bound\\
$\pen$ & $[-40,40]$ & global fixed bound, $\Halt$ only\\
$\pee$ & $[-40,40]$ & global fixed bound, $\Halt$ only\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Frozen production bounds.  They are independent of the injected parameters of the
individual event.}
\end{table}

This point is important because an earlier development configuration used bounds tied to the
injected truth.  A dedicated bounds audit showed that those event-dependent bounds could
artificially raise $\chi^2_{\Hnull}$ and inflate the LRT.  The current production campaign was
therefore run with the broad truth-independent domain above.

\subsection{Known nesting limitation of the current statistic}

The two models are mathematically nested, but the current implementation does not force the
settled $\Hnull$ optimum to be an available $\Halt$ solution.  In particular, for
$\Hnull$-generated events the $\Halt$ fit starts from the generating truth with
$(\pen,\pee)=(0,0)$, not from $(\widehat{\bm\theta}_{0},0,0)$.  A local optimizer can therefore
return $\chi^2_{\Halt}>\chi^2_{\Hnull}$ even though the exact nested problem cannot.

The planned consistency check is to add the embedded settled-null point
\begin{equation}
    (\widehat{\bm\theta}_{0},\pen=0,\pee=0)
\end{equation}
as an explicit $\Halt$ candidate in \emph{both} generated populations and define the final
$\Halt$ objective as the minimum over the current truth-start result and this nested candidate.
This requires refitting but not re-simulation.  Until that check is complete, negative $T$
values are retained and reported as a property of the current frozen procedure rather than
silently clipped.

\section{Empirical null calibration}
% ============================================================

The empirical critical value is defined by the upper tail of the simulated null population:
\begin{equation}
    \widehat c_\alpha
    =
    \widehat F_0^{-1}(1-\alpha),
\end{equation}
with the implementation choosing an observed null value at or above the requested quantile.

The measured thresholds are:
\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{rrrr}
\toprule
$\alpha$ & $c_{\alpha,\chi^2_2}$ & $\widehat c_{\alpha,\Hnull}$ &
$\widehat{\mathrm{FPR}}(c_{\chi^2_2})$\\
\midrule
$5\times10^{-2}$ & 5.991 & 5.742 & 0.043892\\
$10^{-2}$        & 9.210 & 8.946 & 0.008630\\
$10^{-3}$        & 13.816 & 13.160 & 0.000741\\
$10^{-4}$        & 18.421 & 17.867 & 0.000077\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Asymptotic and empirically calibrated critical values.  The current frozen
procedure yields empirical thresholds below the nominal $\chi^2_2$ values; the origin of this
offset is treated as an open validation item rather than a physical conclusion.}
\end{table}

The empirical null distribution is therefore close to, but not identical to, $\chi^2_2$.
The production analysis uses empirical calibration rather than relying solely on the
asymptotic approximation.

The survival comparison makes the direction of the offset explicit: at a fixed candidate
threshold, the empirical $\Hnull$ tail is generally smaller than the nominal $\chi^2_2$ tail.
The increasing jaggedness above roughly $T\sim15$--20 is expected from finite null-sample counts
and is quantified independently by the bootstrap analysis below.

\subsection{Observed offset from the Wilks reference}

The empirical thresholds are systematically below the nominal $\chi^2_2$ values.  The ratios
\begin{equation}
  \widehat c_\alpha/c_{\alpha,\chi^2_2}
  \simeq
  (0.958,\,0.971,\,0.952,\,0.970)
\end{equation}
for $\alpha=(0.05,0.01,10^{-3},10^{-4})$ are suggestive of an approximately global scale
factor near $0.96$.  The current report does \emph{not} interpret this offset as a physical
property of microlensing.  Two concrete explanations remain to be separated: a global mismatch
between injected noise and the error bars used in fitting, and a downward bias of the LRT from
suboptimization of the larger model.

The first check is the $\Hnull$-generated distribution of
$\chi^2_{\Hnull}/(N-k_0)$; the second is to repeat the null calibration after enforcing the
nested $\Hnull\subset\Halt$ construction described in Section~\ref{sec:policy}.  The final paper
should describe the departure from Wilks only after those checks.

\subsection{Physical-variable stability still to be tested}

The completed conditional-stability study is primarily a sampling audit.  For the paper-level
calibration we also need the null false-positive rate at the primary threshold as a function of
physical event variables, in particular $\te$, $u_0$, and the location of $t_0$ relative to the
seasonal observing window.  This is especially important because the measured $\Halt$ power
changes strongly with $\te$; a corresponding trend of the null FPR with timescale would distort
the interpretation of the $(\te,\pi_{\rm E})$ detectability map.

These conditional checks will involve several tens of bins.  Isolated $1$--$2\sigma$ excursions
from the target false-positive rate are therefore expected from multiple comparisons and will
not be interpreted as evidence of instability without a coherent trend or a global test.

\section{Scope of the empirical calibration}
% ============================================================

Cross-validation of the simulated $\Hnull$ sample tests uncertainty in estimating the null
quantile \emph{within the same data-generating model}.  It does not establish that the same
numerical threshold can be transferred unchanged to real LSST photometry.  The present
simulation includes the catalog-based blending prescription used during materialization, but it
does not represent arbitrary blending-model mismatch, time-correlated noise, underestimated or
overestimated survey uncertainties, or other instrumental systematics.

Accordingly, $\widehat c_\alpha$ should be read as the calibrated threshold for this idealized
experiment.  Application to real data would require a separate validation of the photometric
error model and of residual systematics, potentially including an empirical error
renormalization or a data-driven null calibration.

\section{Negative LRT values in the current two-fit policy}
% ============================================================

The compact populations contain:
\begin{align}
    N_{\Hnull}(T<0) &= 1722
    \qquad (1.6568\%),\\
    N_{\Halt}(T<0) &= 2978
    \qquad (0.8925\%).
\end{align}

Because the models are nested, negative values cannot represent the ordering of exact global
minima. They are therefore treated as an optimization/nesting diagnostic. Their relation to
peak coverage, optimizer optimality, active bounds, number of evaluations, and success flags
is analyzed separately.

\subsection{Negative events in the null population}

For $\Hnull$-generated events, poor peak coverage is \emph{not} strongly enriched among
negative-LRT cases. The negative fraction is $1.653\%$ for
\texttt{poor\_peak\_coverage=False} and $1.747\%$ for
\texttt{poor\_peak\_coverage=True}. The continuous sampling diagnostics are also very similar
between negative and non-negative events: the median nearest-peak distance is $0.045\te$ versus
$0.055\te$, and the median number of points within $1\te$ is 21 versus 19. Thus sparse peak
sampling is not the dominant explanation for the negative tail under $\Hnull$.

The clearest difference is numerical. The median optimizer optimality rises from 16.35 to
75.60 for the $\Hnull$ fit and from 23.29 to 415.20 for the $\Halt$ fit in negative events.
The $\Hnull$ fit also requires more function evaluations (median 14 versus 9). Conversely, the
$\Halt$ fit uses fewer evaluations in the negative subset (median 10 versus 15). Both optimizer
success flags are nevertheless true for every event. This demonstrates that the binary success
flag alone is too weak a diagnostic: a formally successful local optimization can still leave
the nested ordering numerically inconsistent. Active parameter bounds are not enriched in the
negative subset and therefore do not appear to be the primary cause.

\subsection{Negative events in the parallax-generated population}

The behavior is qualitatively different for $\Halt$-generated events. Poor peak coverage is a
strong risk factor: the negative fraction is $0.800\%$ when the flag is false and $2.989\%$
when it is true, an increase by a factor of approximately 3.7. The negative subset also has
substantially poorer continuous sampling: its median nearest-peak distance is $0.171\te$
compared with $0.055\te$, the median number of points within $0.5\te$ is 5 instead of 10, and
the median number within $1\te$ is 11 instead of 19. The total number of photometric points is
also much lower (median 127.5 versus 311).

However, only 422 of the 2978 negative $\Halt$ events carry the hard
\texttt{poor\_peak\_coverage} flag. The flag therefore identifies an enriched subgroup but does
not explain the majority of negative cases. The continuous sampling variables contain more
information than the binary threshold. The $\Halt$ fit also takes substantially more function
evaluations in the negative subset (median 25 versus 15), while optimizer success remains true
for all events and active bounds are not enriched. The present evidence therefore points to a
combination of weakly constrained/sparsely sampled event geometry and local optimization-basin
issues rather than a simple bound failure or explicit optimizer failure.

These events are not removed from the empirical null distribution merely because they are
negative: the null calibration is intended to characterize the complete frozen analysis
procedure actually used in production. Their existence is nevertheless an important numerical
diagnostic and motivates direct light-curve and trajectory inspection using the saved CHE data
and fitted parameters, without re-simulation or re-fitting.

\subsection{Correlation-volume and parallax-area scaling with LRT}

Script 13 shows that the dimensionless correlation-volume measure is
non-monotonic with $T$: it increases from the negative-LRT regime to intermediate
positive $T$, then decreases again at very large $T$.  A small
$\det R$ therefore does not uniquely mean ``poor information''; it can also
describe a highly elongated but precisely localized fit.

Script 14 isolates the $2\times2$ parallax covariance block and studies
\begin{equation}
    A_{\rm rel}
    =
    \frac{\sqrt{\det C_{\pi_{\rm E}}}}
         {|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{\rm E}|^2}.
\end{equation}
For positive LRT values this quantity follows an approximately reciprocal
scaling with the LRT statistic.  Representative median
$\log_{10}A_{\rm rel}$ values are approximately $-1.35$ at
$T\simeq95$, $-2.27$ at $T\simeq10^3$, $-3.29$ at $T\simeq10^4$, and

approximately follows
\begin{equation}
    A_{\rm rel}\propto T^{-1},
\end{equation}
consistent with the local quadratic expectation that a linear fractional
uncertainty scale behaves roughly as $T^{-1/2}$.

\subsection{Weak constraint on the finite-source parameter}

The fitted finite-source parameter is much less constrained than the parallax components over
most of the sample.  Representative medians are
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_\rho/\rho \sim 90\text{--}110,
\end{equation}
and the median $\Hnull$--$\Halt$ displacement in $\log_{10}\rho$ is of order
$1.5$--$2.5$ dex depending on LRT region.  At the truth level the median
$\rho/|u_0|$ is only of order $10^{-3}$, so most events are in a regime where finite-source
structure is intrinsically weak.

This does not by itself invalidate the FSPL LRT: $\rho$ is a shared nuisance parameter in both
hypotheses.  It does, however, motivate two explicit pre-paper checks.  First, the occupancy of
the lower and upper $\rho$ bounds should be measured for both hypotheses and across LRT regions,
because boundary accumulation would alter the regularity assumptions behind a $\chi^2_2$
reference.  Second, a PSPL ablation on a representative subset can quantify whether leaving a
nearly unidentified shared $\rho$ free adds material numerical noise to $T$.

\subsection{Amplitude recovery}

Script
\begin{verbatim}
analysis/lrt/characterization/21_piE_amplitude_recovery_vs_truth.py
\end{verbatim}
compares
\begin{equation}
    \Delta\log_{10}\pi_{\rm E}
    =
    \log_{10}
    \left(
    \frac{|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{\rm E}|}
         {|\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\rm E,true}|}
    \right)
\end{equation}
between confused and detected positive-LRT events.

Detected events have a median recovery close to zero over nearly the full
truth range and a comparatively narrow scatter.  Confused events show a strong
positive amplitude bias at low true parallax: near
$\pi_{\rm E,true}\sim0.02$--$0.03$ the median offset is approximately
$0.6$ dex, i.e. a factor of about four in amplitude, and the bias decreases
toward zero as the true parallax increases.

This amplitude bias does not by itself imply a signed bias in either Cartesian
component, because the positive-definite norm of a noisy two-dimensional vector
has a Rice-like upward bias.

\subsection{Monte Carlo propagation of the fitted covariance}

For amplitude uncertainties, script
\begin{verbatim}
analysis/lrt/characterization/22_piE_amplitude_uncertainty_mc_vs_truth.py
\end{verbatim}
draws 1024 samples per event from the frozen bivariate normal approximation
\begin{equation}
    (\pen,\pee)^{(k)}
    \sim
    \mathcal N
    \left(
        \widehat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{\rm E},
        C_{\pi_{\rm E}}
    \right),
\end{equation}
then transforms every draw to
$|\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\rm E}^{(k)}|$.  This is propagation of the frozen local
covariance, not a new light-curve simulation, refit, or likelihood MCMC.

Of $330\,668$ positive events, $330\,659$ have a valid positive-definite
$2\times2$ parallax covariance for this calculation.  Defining
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{68,\rm MC}
    =
    \frac{q_{84}-q_{16}}{2},
\end{equation}
the confused population has
\begin{align}
    q_{16}\!\left(\sigma_{68,\rm MC}/\pi_{\rm E,true}\right) &= 0.570,\\
    \mathrm{median}\!\left(\sigma_{68,\rm MC}/\pi_{\rm E,true}\right) &= 1.855,\\
    q_{84}\!\left(\sigma_{68,\rm MC}/\pi_{\rm E,true}\right) &= 13.075,
\end{align}
and only $31.9\%$ have $\sigma_{68,\rm MC}<\pi_{\rm E,true}$.

For detected events,
\begin{align}
    q_{16} &= 0.0530,\\
    \mathrm{median} &= 0.186,\\
    q_{84} &= 0.681,
\end{align}
and $89.5\%$ have $\sigma_{68,\rm MC}<\pi_{\rm E,true}$.

\subsection{Coverage of the propagated amplitude interval}

68\% over most of the truth range, typically around $0.68$--$0.74$.  Confused

$\pi_{\rm E,true}>q_{84}$, is rare in the same regime.

miss the truth systematically because the distribution is centered at a
positive fitted vector and then transformed through a nonlinear norm.

\subsection{Exact production convention for true parallax components}

The frozen production configuration uses the catalog \texttt{xi} angle with
\begin{verbatim}
angle_basis = galactic_n1n2
component_convention = east_cos_north_sin
\end{verbatim}
The \texttt{alpha} catalog column is retained only as metadata.

The production construction is
\begin{align}
    \pi_{{\rm E},n1} &= \pi_{\rm E}\cos\xi,\\
    \pi_{{\rm E},n2} &= \pi_{\rm E}\sin\xi,
\end{align}
where $n1$ is the local direction of increasing Galactic longitude and $n2$
the local direction of increasing Galactic latitude.  This tangent-plane
vector is then rotated into the local ICRS North/East basis used by pyLIMA.

amplitude-conservation error over the $330\,668$ positive events is

\section{Calibration of the parallax-component covariance}
% ============================================================

For individual Cartesian components no Monte Carlo transformation is needed:
under the frozen multivariate-normal approximation,
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_N=\sqrt{C_{NN}},
    \qquad
    \sigma_E=\sqrt{C_{EE}}
\end{equation}
are the exact marginal standard deviations.

Script 26 shows that confused events have component uncertainties generally of
order or larger than the total true parallax amplitude, while detected events
typically have
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{\pi_{{\rm E},i}}/
    |\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\rm E,true}|
    \sim 0.1\text{--}0.25.
\end{equation}
The East uncertainty is modestly larger than the North uncertainty over much
of the population, consistent with the component-recovery result rather than
indicating an unmodeled component-specific failure.

Script 27 evaluates standardized residuals
\begin{equation}
    z_N=
    \frac{\widehat{\pen}-\pen^{\rm true}}{\sqrt{C_{NN}}},
    \qquad
    z_E=
    \frac{\widehat{\pee}-\pee^{\rm true}}{\sqrt{C_{EE}}}.
\end{equation}
For a calibrated Gaussian marginal,
$P(|z_i|\le1)=0.682689$.  The observed one-sigma coverage is generally
approximately $0.75$--$0.88$ over most of the populated parallax range for both
components and both LRT classes.  The marginal covariances are therefore
conservative rather than underestimated.

Script 28 tests the full two-dimensional covariance through
\begin{equation}
    D_{\pi_{\rm E}}^2
    =
    \Delta\piE^T
    C_{\pi_{\rm E}}^{-1}
    \Delta\piE,
\end{equation}
with the nominal 68\% ellipse defined by
\begin{equation}
    D_{\pi_{\rm E}}^2
    \le
    \chi^2_{2,0.68}
    =
    -2\ln(1-0.68)
    \simeq2.279.
\end{equation}
The empirical 2D coverage is also above the nominal 68\% level through most of
the populated truth range, typically around $0.73$--$0.80$.  Confused and
detected events show broadly similar behavior.  Thus the severe radial
undercoverage found for $|\pi_{\rm E}|$ is not evidence that the Cartesian
parallax covariance is too small; it arises primarily from the nonlinear
positive-definite transformation to amplitude, together with conditioning on
the selected LRT populations.

The origin of the component overcoverage remains part of the calibration audit.  In
particular, it should be compared with the $\chi^2/{\rm dof}$ distribution under $\Hnull$
and with the covariance rescaling convention before it is interpreted as a physical property
of the likelihood geometry.

\section{Finite-source strength and parallax detectability}
% ============================================================

The first finite-source diagnostic uses the continuous truth quantity
\begin{equation}
    R_{\rm FS}
    =
    \frac{\rho^{\rm true}}{|u_0^{\rm true}|}.
\end{equation}
This is preferable to a binary split because only 375 positive-LRT events
satisfy $R_{\rm FS}>1$.

Script
\begin{verbatim}
analysis/lrt/characterization/
    29_detection_fraction_vs_finite_source_strength.py
\end{verbatim}
measures the raw conditional fraction
\begin{equation}
    P(
      \mathrm{parallax\ detected}
      \mid T>0,R_{\rm FS}
    ).
\end{equation}

For $R_{\rm FS}\lesssim1$ the detection fraction changes only moderately around
the overall positive-LRT value.  It declines from roughly the low/mid-$0.7$
range at very small $R_{\rm FS}$ to a broad minimum around $0.64$--$0.66$ near
$R_{\rm FS}\sim10^{-2}$, then returns toward roughly $0.68$--$0.70$ as
$R_{\rm FS}$ approaches unity.  For source-transit events,
$R_{\rm FS}>1$, the measured detection fraction rises, reaching above $0.8$ in
the largest plotted bins, but the Wilson intervals become broad because this
tail contains few events.

This first result does \emph{not} establish a causal finite-source effect.
Before attributing the source-transit rise to $\rho$ itself, the truth
distributions of $\te$ and $|\pi_{\rm E}|$ must be compared across
$R_{\rm FS}$, because both are already known to be strong determinants of
parallax detectability.

\section{Current scientific conclusions}
% ============================================================

The confirmed analysis supports the following statements under the current idealized,
truth-initialized two-fit procedure:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item The empirical null distribution is close to, but systematically thinner than, a
    $\chi^2_2$ reference.  At $\alpha=10^{-3}$ the current empirical threshold is
    $\widehat c=13.1598$.  The approximately constant threshold ratio relative to Wilks is a
    diagnostic to be explained, not yet a physical result.

    \item At the current primary threshold, the population-averaged conditional power in the
    selected $\Halt$ sample is $0.6891$.  Restricting only to strictly positive LRT values gives
    $229\,921$ detected and $100\,747$ confused events, corresponding to a positive-$T$
    detection fraction of $0.6953$.

    \item Annual-parallax detectability depends strongly and jointly on true event timescale and
    true parallax amplitude.  The $(\te,|\pi_{\rm E}|)$ map shows a clear diagonal transition:
    short events require larger parallax amplitudes, while long events can reveal smaller
    amplitudes.

    \item Confused weak-parallax events show a strong positive bias in the scalar amplitude
    $|\widehat{\pi}_{\rm E}|$, but the signed North and East component residuals remain centered
    close to zero.  The amplitude behavior is therefore consistent with the positive-definite
    norm of noisy Cartesian components rather than a large signed bias in either component.

    \item The marginal component covariance and the full two-dimensional parallax covariance
    ellipse are conservative relative to realized component errors.  The poor coverage of the
    central radial interval for $|\pi_{\rm E}|$ should not be interpreted as evidence that the
    Cartesian covariance is too small.

    \item Negative LRT values are a numerical feature of the current independent two-fit
    construction.  They correlate with poorer sampling and stronger selected degeneracies, but
    the mathematically cleaner solution is to enforce the nested $\Hnull$ point explicitly in
    the $\Halt$ candidate set rather than to interpret the negative tail physically.

    \item The first finite-source diagnostic shows only modest variation in parallax
    detectability for $\rho/|u_0|<1$.  The apparent increase for the 375 source-transit events is
    not yet assigned to finite-source physics because $\te$ and $|\pi_{\rm E}|$ must first be
    distribution-matched.
\end{enumerate}

% ============================================================
\section{Practical recommendation for reporting parallax}
% ============================================================

For weak or confused events, the central interval $[q_{16},q_{84}]$ of the propagated scalar
amplitude is not a reliable representation of frequentist coverage.  The preferred primary
representation is therefore the fitted vector
\begin{equation}
    (\pen,\pee)
\end{equation}
with its full $2\times2$ covariance ellipse.  If a one-dimensional amplitude summary is needed
for a non-detection, an upper limit designed specifically for radial coverage is preferable to
treating the central interval of $|\pi_{\rm E}|$ as a Gaussian-like uncertainty.

\section{Open validation items before paper freeze}
\label{sec:open_checks}
% ============================================================

The following items are explicit remaining checks rather than unresolved figure
interpretations.  The existing results remain useful as the baseline against which these tests
will be compared.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item \textbf{Enforce exact nesting and harmonize the test statistic.}  Add the settled
    $\Hnull$ optimum embedded at $(\pen,\pee)=(0,0)$ as an explicit $\Halt$ candidate in both
    generated populations, and use the same coordinate convention for corresponding fits in the
    two generated samples.  Then recalibrate $\widehat c_\alpha$ and remeasure power.  This
    needs refits but no new light-curve simulations.

    \item \textbf{Explain the approximately global offset from Wilks.}  Measure the
    $\Hnull$-generated distribution of $\chi^2_{\Hnull}/(N-k_0)$ and repeat the null LRT after
    the nesting fix.  These tests separate a global noise/error-scale effect from systematic
    suboptimization of $\Halt$.

    \item \textbf{Audit $\rho$ against its production bounds.}  Quantify lower/upper-bound
    occupancy for $\Hnull$ and $\Halt$ and across LRT regions.  If $\rho$ is effectively
    unidentified but not boundary-dominated, it is a weak shared nuisance parameter; if it
    accumulates at a boundary, the regularity assumptions behind a $\chi^2_2$ reference need to
    be reconsidered.  A small PSPL ablation will test whether free $\rho$ adds material
    numerical noise to the LRT.

    \item \textbf{Test physical-variable stability of the null FPR.}  At the primary threshold,
    measure FPR versus $\te$, $u_0$, and peak epoch relative to the seasonal observing window.
    This is required before the $(\te,\pi_{\rm E})$ power map is interpreted as free of
    null-calibration structure.

    \item \textbf{Control the finite-source tail non-parametrically.}  Compare the 375
    $\rho/|u_0|>1$ events with a control population reweighted or matched in
    $(\te,|\pi_{\rm E}|)$.  The spline/logistic adjustment explored during development is not
    retained as a confirmed result because the matching question can be answered more
    transparently without a model-dependent regression.

    \item \textbf{Define transfer to real data separately.}  The current empirical threshold
    calibrates an idealized simulation with known errors and no unmodeled systematics.  A real
    LSST application will require validation of error normalization, time-correlated residuals,
    blending mismatch, and other survey systematics rather than direct reuse of the present
    threshold by assumption.
\end{enumerate}

\statusbox{Interpretation for coauthors.}{The current numbers are not being hidden or discarded:
they are the results of a precisely defined frozen experiment.  The items above identify which
parts must be rechecked before promoting the threshold and power to final paper values.  The
truth-level detectability trends and the component-level parallax characterization are already
useful scientific results, while the exact numerical null calibration remains the main
methodological item to finalize.}
